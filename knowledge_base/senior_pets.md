# Senior Pet Care Guidelines

## When a Pet Is "Senior"
- Dogs: typically 7+ years (large breeds may be senior at 5-6).
- Cats: typically 10+ years.

## Exercise Adjustments
- Senior dogs: shorter, more frequent walks (15-20 min, 2-3x daily).
- Avoid high-impact activities (jumping, long runs).
- Swimming is excellent low-impact exercise for senior dogs.
- Senior cats still need play, but shorter sessions and softer toys.

## Joint and Mobility Care
- Arthritis is very common in senior pets.
- Provide soft bedding and ramps for furniture/stairs.
- Joint supplements (glucosamine, omega-3s) may help — consult vet.
- Watch for stiffness, reluctance to jump, or limping.

## Feeding Senior Pets
- Switch to senior-formula food around age 7 (dogs) or 10 (cats).
- Senior pets often need fewer calories but more protein.
- Smaller, more frequent meals are easier to digest.
- Monitor weight closely — both weight loss and gain can signal illness.

## Medication Schedules
- Senior pets often need daily medications (joint, thyroid, heart).
- Consistency is critical — set reminders.
- Never miss vet checkups; senior pets benefit from biannual visits.

## Cognitive Health
- Senior dogs and cats can develop cognitive decline (similar to dementia).
- Signs: confusion, disrupted sleep, accidents in the house.
- Mental enrichment and routine help slow cognitive decline.
