# Pet Medication Guidelines

## Timing and Consistency
- Give medications at the same time every day.
- Most oral medications are easier with food unless the label says otherwise.
- Set phone alarms to avoid missed doses.

## Common Medication Categories
- **Heartworm preventatives**: monthly, usually a chewable.
- **Flea/tick preventatives**: monthly topical or oral.
- **Antibiotics**: finish the entire course even if pet seems better.
- **Pain/anti-inflammatory (NSAIDs)**: always with food, never combine with steroids.
- **Thyroid medication**: typically twice daily, on an empty stomach for cats.

## Safety Rules
- Never give human medications (Tylenol, Advil, ibuprofen) to pets — many are toxic.
- Store medications out of reach; chocolate-flavored chewables can attract pets.
- If a dose is missed, do NOT double the next dose; consult the vet.
- Watch for side effects: vomiting, lethargy, loss of appetite, unusual behavior.

## Giving Pills
- Hide in a small piece of cheese, peanut butter (xylitol-free), or pill pocket.
- For cats, gentle restraint and a pill popper tool may work best.
- Always confirm the pill was swallowed — cats are masters at spitting them out.

## Emergency Signs
- Difficulty breathing, severe vomiting, seizures, or collapse after medication = vet ER immediately.
- Keep your vet's number AND a 24-hour pet poison hotline saved.
